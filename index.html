A little care goes a long way

Food has a journey. Let’s make it count.

From a well-planned kitchen to a community meal, small decisions can help good food reach people instead of becoming waste. Explore the moments that make food rescue meaningful.

01 / PLAN WITH PURPOSE

Buy thoughtfully. Store carefully.

Planning portions and using food in time can prevent avoidable waste at home and in kitchens.

02 / RESPECT EVERY INGREDIENT

Good food deserves a plan.

Check what you already have, label leftovers and make a meal plan before shopping again.

03 / SHARE THE SURPLUS

Connect before the clock runs out.

Clear quantities, storage details and collection deadlines help potential partners assess a food offer.

04 / MAKE EVERY MEAL MATTER

Less waste. More care.

Food rescue works best when safe handling, timely collection and trusted community partners come first.

Understand the impact

Food waste is more than food in a bin.

When food is discarded, the land, water, energy, labour and transport used to produce it are wasted too. Reducing avoidable waste is a practical step towards more responsible consumption.

01

The global picture

1.05 billion tonnes

Food waste across households, food services and retail in 2022, according to UNEP’s Food Waste Index Report 2024.

Read the UNEP report ↗
02

Plan before you buy

Check your fridge and pantry, make a shopping list, and buy quantities you can use. Flexible meals can help use ingredients before they spoil.

03

Store food wisely

Follow storage instructions, refrigerate perishable food promptly, label leftovers and use older items first. When in doubt about safety, do not donate or serve it.

04

Share responsibly

Offer only food that has been handled safely. State preparation time, storage conditions, allergens, quantity and a realistic collection deadline.

A simple habit for today

Pause before you throw it away.

Can it be planned into another meal, stored safely, or shared in time with someone who can use it? Choose the safest practical option.

Why this matters

The missing piece is often the connection.

Edible surplus can be difficult to redirect when a donor does not know who is nearby, who can collect it, or how quickly it needs to move. FoodLoop aims to make those details easier to share.

01 / THE CHALLENGE

Good food can go unused

Events, kitchens, shops and households can have safe, edible surplus that needs a clear next step.

02 / THE GAP

Coordination takes effort

Donors and NGOs need practical details: quantity, location, timing, handling and a reliable way to speak.

03 / THE IDEA

Make the handover easier

A shared listing can help community partners assess an offer and coordinate collection before the pickup window closes.

A straightforward process

Three steps. One useful connection.

The process should be easy to understand for first-time donors and practical for organisations that collect and distribute food.

STEP 01＋

Share the details

Describe the food, quantity, preparation time, pickup deadline, location and any allergens or storage requirements.

STEP 02↔

Coordinate collection

An NGO or community partner reviews the information and contacts the donor to agree on logistics.

STEP 03✓

Confirm the handover

The partner arranges safe transport and distribution, then records the outcome.

For food donors

Have surplus? Start with the details.

Share an accurate offer so a potential collecting partner can judge whether the quantity, location and pickup window are workable.

  • Describe the food and quantity honestly.
  • Set a realistic pickup deadline.
  • List known allergens and storage needs.
  • Do not offer food you suspect is unsafe.
